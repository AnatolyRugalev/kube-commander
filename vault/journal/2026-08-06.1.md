## Why "canonical" is a fact about the board, not a preference

Both readings in BOARD-02's notes sounded equally defensible, so the way in was to count
rather than argue. 229 entries are in the index today; **207 of them appear nowhere else on
the board**. Their sections are gone — `### M0 — Groundwork` is one line reading `_(none — M0
complete)_`, M1's and M2's are a sentence each. Only 8 ids appear on both sides, and those 8
are the currently-open lines.

That settles it in one direction. The per-line `- [x]` cannot be canonical, because the board
has already thrown away 207 of them and nobody noticed — which is correct behaviour: a leg
that closes the SEARCH line and replaces four `- [x]` lines with "the line is closed" has done
exactly what the board wants. What makes it safe is that those four entries are in the index.
When they are not, the same tidy-up is a silent deletion, and it looks identical in the diff.

The 26 that were adrift were one such tidy-up away. BOX-01…03 close the BOX line; the next leg
to write `_(none — the BOX line is closed)_` would have taken them.

## Why this is a copy and not the backfill BOARD-01 refused

BOARD-01's sentence is the one worth quoting back: "eight summaries written by a leg that did
not do the work is how a wrong one gets in." That is right, and if it still applied this leg
should have stopped.

It stopped applying because of what BOARD-01 itself did. Its collapse pass rewrote every
paragraph entry on the board into the D102 one-line shape — including the entries sitting in
the *open* lines' sections, which is the population that was adrift. So by the time this leg
ran, 25 of the 26 were already exactly a Done entry: `- [x] **AUTH-03** Recognise an expired
AWS SSO session, and name the profile — done 2026-08-04 (D212)`. Moving that is not writing a
summary, it is `cp`. Several were wrapped across two source lines by the 90-column margin, and
re-joining a wrapped line is the only edit 25 of them took.

The 26th, `M2-07`, is the case D225 pt 2 and pt 3 exist for: a parent from July whose section
entry is a title plus a 28-line `notes:` block, and whose four slices M2-07a…d are all already
indexed. Its index line reuses its own title verbatim, its own `done: 2026-07-20`, and the
four `(Dnn)` its notes name, and says "via its four slices" — it makes no claim about what
landed that the slices do not already carry. `PAL-03` is the same shape and got the same
treatment; its old text said "done 2026-08-02 via its two slices, **PAL-03a** (D198) and
**PAL-03b** (D199)", which the D102 regex rejects because the tail after the date is not a
bare `(Dnn, …)`, so it became `…, via its two slices — done 2026-08-02 (D198, D199)`. Both
pointers survive; that is the rule the whole pass ran under.

## The guard runs in one direction, deliberately

`TestBoardDoneIndexIsComplete` asserts every `- [x]` above `## Done` has an entry below it. The
reverse — every index entry has a section — is not checked and must not be: 207 entries would
fail it on the first run, and the index outliving its section is the entire reason the index
exists.

The one-directional shape also matches where the mistake happens. Nobody adds an index entry
for work that did not happen; the failure is a leg finishing its work, ticking its own section
line, and closing the board. So the test speaks in exactly those terms — it names the id, the
line number of the section entry, and tells the reader to add *the same line, unchanged*,
which is the instruction D225 pt 2 wants followed and is what makes it a copy the next time
too.

It joins the two BOARD-01 guards for the reason D224 pt 3 gives: this rule had been true by
convention for three weeks, was written in nobody's checklist, and broke for two dozen legs
running. The version of this leg that just backfills is the version that gets to be done again
in September.
