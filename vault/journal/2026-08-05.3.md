## The filter field is the third text field, and it gets the same four keys

`routeFilterKey` applies the D73 control/text split: a mapped key with `Text == ""` is a
control action, anything else is input for the field. So the honest set falls out of one line
of code rather than out of `handleFilterAction`'s switch — the switch also honours top/bottom
and both paging pairs, and those are omitted here exactly as they are omitted in every other
context. What is left is move (the selection steps through the live-narrowed rows, so a match
can be previewed while typing), enter, esc: the same four as `HelpPickerFilter` and
`HelpPrompt`. Three text fields, one rule, three contexts — the sets coincide today, and
sharing one context would be the wrong economy, because what makes them equal is that each
router happens to apply the same split, not that they are the same surface.

The esc question the board flagged — its two meanings in sequence, clear then close — turned
out not to be a hint question at all. `nav.back` acts either way, and a hint names a key that
acts; which of the two things it does is the field's state, and spelling that out is copy the
registry has no room for (D218 pt 2). Worth noting the asymmetry it leaves: with the field
open, esc clears *and* closes, so `enter` (commit, keeping the narrowing) is the only way to
keep a filter — which the hint does advertise, and which is why drill-in stays in the set even
though "Open / drill into selection" is a poor gloss for "commit".

The added test types `s` into the open field and asserts the hint does not move. That is the
one assertion in the pair that is about the *lie* rather than the set: `s` is `sort.column` on
the table underneath, the browse hint advertised it, and in the field it types a character.

## The panel is the only capturing surface with no text field

Everything else HINT-01/02/03 covered either captures text (five surfaces) or is a pager
(logs, viewer, overlay). The port-forward panel is neither: it routes resolved actions through
`handleForwardsPanelAction`, so its set is simply that switch — cursor, stop the selected
forward, stop all, and the ways out. That makes it the only context whose set is a transcript
of a handler rather than a judgement about which keys survive a field, and the only one where
"what acts" and "what is worth hinting" barely differ; `forwards.panel` (`F`, which also
closes) is the single omission, for the reason `q` is omitted from the confirm set.

`nav.drillIn` stops a forward and is hinted "Open / drill into selection". I left that rather
than lengthening the description, and D218 pt 2 records why: descriptions are global, the `?`
overlay renders them in a fixed column (D147 pt 3), and a per-context description would be a
second source of copy competing with the registry (D11). The panel's own footer already says
"enter: stop" one line above the hint line.

That footer — `enter: stop · X: stop all · esc: close` — is the one thing I considered
changing and did not. It spells literal keys inside a view, which no other surface does and
which a rebind silently breaks. Removing it is tempting now that the hint line carries the
same keys generated, but it is the *only* place the panel's verbs are stated, and deleting it
to fix a D11 wrinkle would trade a real lie (wrong keys after a rebind) for a different one
(a hint that says "open" where the key stops something). The fix that costs nothing is to
generate the footer's keys from the keymap while keeping its verbs, which is its own small
item — filed on the board rather than folded in here, since it is a view change with its own
tests and this leg is about the hint line. HINT-02 set the precedent for leaving it: the
keybindings overlay's body lists every binding in the app and was hinted anyway.

## Reading the router is what makes the set closed

Both cases went in at the position their router tests them — `m.filtering` between the pickers
and the modals in `Update`, `m.forwardsPanel` after the viewer and before the help overlay in
`handleAction` — and after writing them the two switches read as the same list in the same
order. That is the property worth keeping, and it is the whole of D218 pt 1: nothing enforces
it. `contextShortHelpActions` is a map with no exhaustiveness check, `ShortHelpContext` falls
back silently, and a new capturing surface with no case compiles, passes every test, and ships
a plausible wrong hint. The defence is that the enumeration is auditable by reading the
routers rather than by trusting the board — which is how these two were found in the first
place, and why D217 pt 1 could promise there was no third.
