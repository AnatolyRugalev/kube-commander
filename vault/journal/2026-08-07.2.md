## The two asks read as one sentence, which is why the line had to be drawn first

The feedback's own words are "I want switching contexts to feel like window/tab switching in
an OS", and the reason that sentence is hard to schedule is that it describes two independent
properties that happen to arrive together in a real tab bar: a tab switch is *instant*, and a
tab *comes back the way you left it*. CTX-WARM is the first. This feedback is mostly the
second, and it says so — the list is "which resource type I had open, where I'd drilled in,
scroll position", not milliseconds.

That distinction is worth more than tidiness because the two halves have different gates.
D196 pt 5 holds every slice that changes what a switch retains behind
`2026-07-29-context-switch-live-dogfood` pts 3-6, and the reason is specific: those are the
leak checks, and optimising a teardown before you know the teardown is sound measures two
moving parts. That reasoning is entirely about **live** things — a retained client, a warm
discovery result, and the nightmare case D196 pt 1 names, "a retained *watch* firing into a
dead view".

A remembered GVR cannot fire into anything. So the honest reading is that D196 pt 5's gate
does not reach this half, and the feedback — which the submitter thought pushed against
D196 — turns out to be buildable now while the half nobody asked twice for stays waiting on
a human. Had I filed the whole thing under CTX-WARM, as the shared word "context switch"
invites, it would have inherited a gate that has nothing to say about it and sat behind an
open dogfood indefinitely.

## The submitter offered a bound, and the right answer was to decline it

The feedback pre-conceded the hard part: "It's fine for this to be bounded rather than
unconditional: evict … after some idle time, or once more than N contexts are held in
memory. I don't need infinite history."

That is a generous constraint to be handed, and D240 pt 5 declines it — not to take more,
but because it is the wrong shape once the retention stops being in memory. Idle-eviction
and an N-cap are answers to "how much am I holding", and the answer here is a GVR string in
a file that already exists per context, sitting next to `lastNamespace`, which nobody has
ever proposed evicting. Writing an eviction policy for it would be code that can only ever
be wrong: too aggressive and it forgets a context the reader still uses, too lax and it does
nothing at all, and either way it guards a resource that is not scarce.

The bound the submitter was reaching for is real, though — it just belongs to the other
half, where it already exists and is stricter than what they asked for: D196 pt 4 caps live
retention at the **previous context only**, one entry. So the answer to "bound it by N" is
"the live half is bounded at N=1 and stays there; the remembered half has nothing to bound",
and both halves of that sentence are already written down.

## Why the address, and not the rows

The tempting version of "come back to what I was looking at" retains the table — the rows
were already fetched, and re-fetching them is exactly the latency the *other* half of the
feedback complains about. It is also the one thing that must not happen. Rows from a cluster
you are not connected to are stale the moment they are stored, and there is no correct
refresh story for them (the same argument D165 pt 1 made when it returned a *scope* rather
than a `[]Row`, and D155 pt 3 before it). Storing the address instead means the restore is
indistinguishable from the reader having pressed the keys themselves: a fresh watch on a
fresh connection, with the seed menu underneath it.

That also settles where the state lives without a new mechanism. `handleClusterConnected`
already replays `msg.state.Namespace` after `resetCluster`, having installed
`msg.state.MenuExtras` before it, and the shape of the whole thing is a value with no client
in it, computed off the update loop, discarded when the connect failed. A resource is the
same kind of value as a namespace and needs no new lifecycle — which is what makes CTX-MEM-02
a small slice rather than an architecture.

## The one thing CTX-MEM-02 must not ship without

A restore that cannot fail politely is a downgrade, and this is the failure that will
actually happen: contexts differ in what they serve. Pin `externalsecrets` on the cluster
that has the operator, switch to the one that does not, and a naive replay drills into a kind
that is not there — producing, at best, the CRD-01 empty pane with a LIST error on a screen
the reader did not ask for it on, on every switch, forever.

So D240 pt 3 makes the degrade a property of the line rather than a bug to be found later:
the restore is a convenience and must never be the reason a switch shows an error. If the
kind is not in the new cluster's discovered set, you land where you land today — seed menu,
welcome pane — and nothing is said. That also keeps the D196 pt 4 invariant that matters
most here: nothing user-visible may *depend* on a context having been visited before. A
second visit may start closer to where you were; it may not be a different app.
