## Three pre-existing tests assert things this leg makes untrue

Worth naming, because "the leg changed a test" is the shape of a leg quietly loosening a
guard, and none of these three is that.

`TestConfirmContextResolution` asserted `y` is **unbound in the browse context**, with the
reason in its comment: `res.yaml` was retired into `res.edit` (D135/M3-15c) and left the
key free. The board brief for this leg saw the collision coming and said to "bind
deliberately rather than discovering the overlap later", so the assertion is now that `y`
resolves to `logs.yank` in browse and `confirm.accept` in confirm. That is a *stronger*
test of the context split than the old one: `n`/`enter`/`esc` are twinned but their browse
meanings are inert while a modal is up, whereas `y` is now two live actions told apart by
nothing but which surface owns input.

`TestMergeOverride` rebound `app.filter` onto `v` as "a free, non-nav key" and now collides
with `logs.select`. It moved to `x`. Nothing about the merge is being tested less.

`TestShortHelpContext` asserted `logs.regex` is hinted in **both** logs contexts. It is now
hinted only with the grep open — see below.

## The hint line is a fixed budget, and I had to spend from it

Adding two entries to `HelpLogs` made the rendered hint elide at 300 columns, which the
existing `TestLogsHintBarTracksGrepState` caught immediately: `logs.wrap`, `nav.back` and
`app.quit` fell off the end behind the `…`. That test was written to pin promises made
before this leg, and quietly letting them break is how a hint stops being a promise
(D143 pt 1).

So something had to go, and the same test file already contains the argument for which.
`logs.timestamps` is hinted in neither context, and its comment says why: "the closed-grep
hint line is already the scarcest in the app (six entries elide at 220 columns)". Against
that budget, `logs.regex` with the grep *closed* is the weakest entry on the line — there
is no query for the mode to re-interpret, and the action's own doc comment says the mode
"matters least" before typing. `v` and `y` are the opposite case: a copy gesture is
invisible until someone tells you it exists, and the feedback that asked for this feature
opened by explaining that the reporter's only known route to copying a log line was to drop
mouse capture and use the terminal.

The regex toggle keeps its `HelpLogsFilter` slot, where the query it acts on exists, keeps
firing in both states, and keeps its `[re]` header marker. I also shortened both new
descriptions ("Select log lines (visual mode)", "Copy the selected log lines") — they are
what `?` and `docs/keybindings.md` print, and the long forms cost the line two more entries
for no extra meaning.

## `G` is the only nav key whose meaning had to change

LOGS-04c made `nav.bottom` mean "catch up *and keep up*" (D147), and it is a good rule: in
a streaming pager the bottom is not a position. Inside a selection it is the wrong rule, and
not marginally — re-arming follow pins the cursor to the newest line (D242 pt 5), so `G`
would drag the moving end of the selection along with the stream and the reader would be
selecting the tail rather than what they are looking at.

The board's own text is what settles it. LOGS-SEL-03 asks whether "a *yank everything
visible* shortcut earns a binding or `gg v G y` is enough" — which only parses if `G` in
visual mode extends to the end. So it does, and follow stays off. This is the one
mode-dependent nav key in the view, which is worth stating plainly because D143 pt 1 treats
mode-dependence as a reason *not* to advertise something: `nav.left`/`nav.right` are hinted
in neither logs context precisely because they only act while unwrapped.

`logs.follow` went the other way. It is unambiguous — "back to the stream" — and following
owns the cursor, so it ends the selection rather than coexisting with it. The alternative,
making `f` inert inside visual mode, would have left a reader who wants the tail back with
no key that gives it to them except esc-then-`f`.

## Seven mutations, and the one that passed was masked by a clamp

1. `startSelect` does not pause following → 1 failure.
2. `endSelect` never resumes → 1 failure.
3. Drop the anchor re-find in `rebuildShown` → **passed**.
4. `nav.bottom` re-arms follow while selecting → 1 failure.
5. `Yank` reads `shownLines` (the painted cache) instead of `lines` → 2 failures.
6. Drop the anchor clamp in `placeCursor` → 1 failure.
7. `logs.follow` does not end the selection → 1 failure.

Mutation 3 is the CTX-MEM-02 journal's lesson arriving again in a different costume: *a
guard masked by another guard in the test's setup is untested.* The first version of
`TestSelectionKeepsItsLogLinesAcrossAFilterChange` selected the last two lines of a
five-line buffer and greped them down to two. Deleting the re-find left the anchor at a
stale index of 3 — and `placeCursor`'s clamp, which exists for a completely different
reason, pulled it to 1, which is the answer the re-find would have produced. The test was
green either way.

The two disagree only when the anchor is **not** the last shown line, because that is the
only time a stale index is still in range. Rewritten with the anchor in the middle of the
buffer and a matching line after it, deleting the re-find widens the selection by one line
— it yanks an `err c` the reader never selected — and the test fails. That is also the real
failure mode: a selection that silently grows when you type into the grep would put a line
on someone's clipboard that they did not choose, which is the worst thing a copy feature can
do quietly.

Mutation 5 is the one the feedback predicted in writing ("no styling in the clipboard …
the kind of thing that looks right on screen and only shows up once the text is pasted
somewhere"), and it fails two tests rather than one, because the wrapped-line test catches
it independently: `shownLines` holds the line unwrapped too, but painted, so the escape
assertion and the whole-line assertion are genuinely separate claims.
