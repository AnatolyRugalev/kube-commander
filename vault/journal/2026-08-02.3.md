## Why PAL-03 had to be split, and where the seam is

PAL-03 as written is two different problems wearing one id. Committing a verb in place and
swapping the item list is a *surface* change, entirely inside `palette.go` plus three small
accessors on the picker. Offering `namespace` or `context` as an argument is a *plumbing*
change: both verbs' values arrive later, in `namespacesLoadedMsg`/`contextsLoadedMsg`, whose
handlers today seed one named picker and bail unless **that** picker is still open —

```go
if !m.nsPicker.Active() { return m, nil }
```

— so the palette cannot be the surface waiting for them without teaching those loads who is
asking, and without deciding what the box shows in the second before the answer lands. Doing
both at once would have been one leg with two designs in it, and the second design (a
pending/empty argument list) is the one that deserves its own thinking. So this slice took
the mechanism and the two verbs whose values need no plumbing at all: the resource kinds are
a snapshot of the menu the shell already holds, and the themes are compiled in. Both were
already computed by their own `open*Picker`, so wiring them was extraction, not new logic —
which is exactly the property D198 pt 2 now requires of every future argument verb.

## The exception to D197, and the fence around it

D197 pt 1 says the palette resolves to an `Action` and dispatches it through `handleAction`.
Taken literally, PAL-03 cannot exist: dispatching `resources.switch` opens the resource
picker **over** the palette, which is the pile of separate modals the feedback asked to be
rid of. So an argument verb now stops short of dispatch and commits into the stage instead.

That is a real weakening, and it would rot in the obvious way — each argument verb growing
its own little copy of what the verb does — so D198 pt 2 fences it: the stage's apply must
be *the same function* the verb's standalone picker ends in. `resource` already had one
(`selectResource`, shared with the menu drill-in). `theme` did not: its apply was the tail
of `handleThemeSelected`, inline after the label lookup, so this leg split out
`applyThemeNamed` and both surfaces now call it. The rule that matters for the next slice is
that this extraction is *part of adding an argument verb*, not a tidy-up someone may skip:
skipping it is how the palette's `:theme ` and `T` would come to disagree about whether
picking your current theme costs a file write.

The same instinct produced `resourcePickerItems`: the palette's argument list and `R`'s
picker are now one snapshot function, so the two surfaces cannot come to offer different
kinds — the failure mode where a CRD discovered late shows up in one list and not the other.

## Space had to become a separator, and that is a real cost

The line only works if something commits the verb mid-typing, and space is the key every
`:`-line in every tool uses for it. But the palette's items are *sentences* — "Switch
resource", "Clear sort (restore order)" — so space was previously usable query text, and
taking it is a loss that had to be checked rather than waved past:

- The matcher is `kube.NameMatcher`, whose subsequence fallback skips characters freely, so
  `switchr` and `clearsort` still find their rows. Nothing became unreachable.
- The alternative — space types unless the highlighted verb takes an argument — makes one
  key sometimes-text and sometimes-a-commit depending on a row the reader may not be
  looking at. A separator that is *always* a separator is the predictable one, so a space
  on a plain verb is simply swallowed (pinned by `TestPaletteSpaceOnAPlainVerbIsSwallowed`,
  which asserts the query and the selection are both untouched — a swallowed key must not
  move the list either).
- Inside an argument the rule reverts: space is text again. No Kubernetes name contains
  one, so this costs nothing today, and it keeps the rule "the separator separates the two
  halves of the line" rather than "space is special in this box".

`enter` commits too, and deliberately: it is what the palette already trained on the verb
list, and a reader who does not know about space must not hit a dead end.

## What the reader sees, and the two states that had to be made distinguishable

Rendered at 80×24, the stage is legible from the prompt alone — `:res` in the verb stage,
`:resource ` once committed, `:resource cron` while narrowing — which is why the prompt
became a picker capability (`SetPrompt`) rather than the palette painting its own line: the
filter field is where the cursor is, so the stage has to be readable *there*, not only in
the title. The title changes too (`Command` → `Switch resource`), so the two signals agree.

`ClearQuery` exists for one sharp edge worth naming: `SetItems` applies whatever query is
standing, so swapping the item list without clearing first would have shown the *resource*
list filtered by the word "resource" — a stage that opens on two matches and looks broken.
It is the swap-in-place counterpart to Hide/Show, and any future surface that replaces its
items while staying on screen needs it.

## Two things noticed, left alone deliberately

- **The hint bar still lies while a picker is open** — it shows the browse hints while every
  key types into the palette. PAL-02 flagged it and suggested this leg pick it up; it is
  still the right fix (a picker `HelpContext`, the shape D143 pt 1 used for the logs view)
  and still a leg of its own — it touches the hint bar, not the palette, and this leg's diff
  was already at its limit. It is now *more* worth doing: the palette's line has two
  gestures (space, backspace) that nothing on screen advertises.
- **`R`, `T` and the standalone pickers are untouched.** Converting them is PAL-05's job and
  only PAL-05's (D194 pt 4/D197 pt 3), and until PAL-03b lands they are still the only route
  to a namespace or a context. This slice adds a way in; it takes none away.
