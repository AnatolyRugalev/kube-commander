## Why this was not "add a syncHints call to the picker openers"

That is the fix the four journal entries flagging this bug implied, and it is the fix that
would have re-introduced it. Counting the sites is what settles it: `Show()` is called on
a picker in 8 places and `Hide()` in about 20 — the eight openers, the two selection/cancel
arms in `Update`, `resetForContextSwitch` hiding all seven at once, and each handler that
closes its own picker on the way to acting. Every one of them is a place where an author
who has never read D143 must remember an unrelated call, and the hint has *no* failure
mode that shows up in a test they would write: it renders, it is just wrong.

The derivation costs one type switch and one comparison per message, and it makes the
class of bug unreachable rather than fixed: a future overlay that captures input gets a
case in `hintContext` and is correct at every site that opens or closes it, including the
ones that do not exist yet. `Update` is renamed to `update` with a five-line wrapper, which
is the whole diff of the mechanism.

The forced `syncHints` did not go away and should not: a resize re-elides the *same*
context to a new width, which the memo cannot see. That is the shape of the split — the
tail sync answers "who owns input now", the explicit call answers everything else.

## The order the derivation reads in is not cosmetic

`hintContext` resolves search → logs-grep → picker → logs-view → pane focus, which is
exactly the order `Update` routes a `KeyPressMsg` in. It has to be, because the hint is a
claim about which keys act and the router is what decides that. The case worth naming: the
picker sits **above** the logs view but **below** the logs grep, because that is where
`routePickerKey` sits, and both orderings are reachable — the container picker opens over a
table on the way to the logs view, and `m.logsView.Filtering()` is checked before any
picker in the router. Splitting `m.logsView.Active()` into its two states (rather than
nesting the grep check inside one case, as the old `syncHints` did) is what let the picker
case land between them.

## What the two picker contexts deliberately omit

Paging (`ctrl+d`/`ctrl+u`) acts in an open picker and is not hinted, for the reason every
other context omits it: the hint is the keys a reader must be *told* about, not an
inventory of what the surface consumes.

The port picker's own two gestures — `p` (prompt for a local port) and `0` (let the OS
pick one, D139) — act with its field closed and are not hinted either, and this one is a
constraint rather than a preference (D206 pt 3). `HelpPicker` is shared by any picker in
the closed-field state; a set naming `p` and `0` would be a lie in the next opt-in picker
that does not bind them. A picker that wants its own gestures on the hint line needs its
own context, and should probably justify why it is not simply a full-screen view.

## Two neighbours left alone, on purpose

- **The confirm modal and the prompt modal have the same defect.** `routeModalConfirmKey`
  swallows everything but `y`/`n`/esc/enter, and `routeModalPromptKey` captures text, yet
  the hint under them is still the browse set. It is the identical shape (two contexts,
  two cases in `hintContext`) and it is a separate leg because the honest set is a
  separate question: the confirm keymap is its own `ConfirmAction` table (D132), so the
  hint would have to be sourced from it rather than from the browse registry.
- **The help overlay and the shared viewer** likewise capture input while they are up.
  The overlay is arguably self-explanatory (it *is* the key list) and the viewer's hint
  is a smaller lie, but neither is right. Both belong in the same follow-up item as the
  modals, and the derivation now makes each of them a one-case change.
