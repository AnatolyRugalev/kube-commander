## What this slice was actually about

PAL-05a's entry made the case for splitting PAL-05 per key by counting references. That
was the right call and this leg confirms the size — but it undersold what the second
conversion would show, which is not cost but **inheritance**.

`R`'s picker was the surface CRD-PIN-04 hardened, and hardened for a real reason: on a
CRD-heavy cluster a kind is reached by typing what `kubectl` takes (`externalsecrets`,
`es`, the group), and two operators' `Cluster` used to collapse to one unpickable row.
All of that is D203, and *none of it moved in this leg*. It did not have to: PAL-03a had
already seeded the `:resource ` stage from `resourcePickerItems`, the same snapshot the
picker used, precisely so the two could not drift. So retiring the picker deleted the
second place those guarantees had to hold while leaving the guarantees intact — which is
the argument for the whole PAL-05 line stated as a diff rather than as a principle.

The gain in the other direction is the line: `R` now backspaces to the verb list. That
matters more here than it did for `T`, because the resource stage is the one you can
legitimately fail to find what you want in — a kind that discovery has not listed is not
there, and the reader's next move is `:pin ` or `:search `, both one backspace away
instead of an esc and a new key.

## The two `handleAction` arms became one, on purpose

`case keymap.ActionTheme, keymap.ActionResources: return m.openPaletteArg(a)`. The
alternative — one arm per key, each with its own comment naming its slice — is what the
mechanical conversion produces, and it is worse for a specific reason: it implies the
keys differ. They do not. What a converted key *is* is entirely contained in D207 pt 1
(the key names the verb, `enterPaletteArg` produces the stage), so per-key arms would be
four copies of one fact, and the fourth would be the one that quietly grew a special
case. PAL-05c adds two names to the arm and no code.

`*` (pin) is deliberately **not** joining that arm even though `pin` is an argument verb.
Its key reads the cursor and toggles the kind under it (D204); only the *palette's* pin
verb takes a kind by name. Two different gestures that happen to share a verb — the
conversion rule is about keys whose meaning is "choose a value for this verb", and `*`'s
is not.

## What moved in the tests, and the one thing that did not

`respicker_test.go` is now `resourcestage_test.go`, and its six tests are re-pointed
rather than rewritten: `press(t, m, capitalR)` + `m.resPicker` became `openResourceStage`
+ `m.cmdPicker`, and the one `picker.SelectedMsg{Kind: resourcePickerKind}` became
`commandPickerKind`. That is D207 pt 4 — what those tests pin (aliases, group
qualification, resolution to the right GVR) is a property of `resourcePickerItems`, which
this leg did not touch, so deleting them with the picker would have narrowed the
guarantee while the diff looked like tidying.

The file was **renamed** where `theme_test.go` was not, and the difference is worth
naming: `theme_test.go` was named for the domain, `respicker_test.go` for the surface. A
test file named after a retired surface is a small lie that survives every future grep.

The one assertion that genuinely changed meaning is in `palette_test.go`:
`TestPaletteOpensOnColon` used to prove `:` opens the palette "and not the resource
picker". There is no resource picker to not open any more, so the same intent is now
`m.palArg == ""` — `:` starts on the verbs, `R` starts on `:resource `. That is the
honest form of PAL-02's original claim once the two surfaces are one.
