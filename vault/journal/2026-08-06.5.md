## What "check it against the code" actually cost, and what it bought

Twenty-odd claims across four paragraphs. Most were true and cheap to confirm — `defaultLogTail
= 1000` is a const in `logs.go`; `PodContainers` returns regular/init/ephemeral and
`ctrPurpose.offer` drops init for exec; `surfaceError` logs on its first line; `browsefail.go`
names the conversion webhook and the 406; `helpContextCount`/`HelpContexts()` exist with a test
on each side; `elide.Lines` is used by both the modal and the help overlay, and
`forwardsPanelTitle` really does count what the window hides. Four sections, maybe twenty
greps.

The two that were not cheap are the two that mattered.

**HINT was stale exactly where the board said it would be** — this is the failure D226 pt 1
named, and it had been sitting between an `- [x] HINT-05` entry and a paragraph saying HINT-05
closed the thing the paragraph above said nothing closed. Both statements were on screen, four
lines apart, for a day. Nobody reading the board top-down would have believed the wrong one for
long; a leg searching for "is the hint enforced?" would have found the wrong one first.

**LOGS was worse, because it was true.** "Closed on cost (D160, D162)" is accurate and
complete about the two costs those slices measured — the initial fetch and the per-line render.
Reading `logsview.appendLine` to confirm the second one is what surfaced the third: `lines`,
`stamps` and `shownLines` are appended to and never trimmed, `Reset` only fires at the next
open, and the 2026-08-01 dogfood measured ~1,900 lines/sec. A view left following a chatty
container grows without bound, and the board's own prose had walked right past it — the
sentence *"a followed stream keeps growing the buffer long after the initial tail"* is in the
LOGS-05 triage, used there as the argument for fixing per-line cost.

That is the second consecutive harvest to produce a real product item (PAL-06, now LOGS-07)
out of a paragraph everyone had read many times. The pattern in both: the prose was written by
the leg that *closed* something, in the vocabulary of what it closed, and the gap next door had
no vocabulary at all.

## Why LOGS keeps its section and the other three lost theirs

D229 pt 1 licenses collapsing a **closed** line only, because an open line's prose is what the
next slice is picked from. LOGS stopped being closed halfway through this leg. The resolution
is the honest one rather than the convenient one: the heading now reads *reopened on memory
(LOGS-07)*, the collapsed history stays collapsed above it (it is all closed work — features
and latency, both genuinely finished), and the one open item carries its own notes, as every
open item does. Collapsing the history did not depend on the line being closed *forever*; it
depended on those particular claims being finished and checked, which they are.

The alternative — leave all 36 lines because a new item appeared — would have made the sweep
self-defeating: the better the check, the more likely it turns something up, and the more prose
survives to hide the next thing.
