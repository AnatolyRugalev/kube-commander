## Why the guard changed rather than gained an exemption

The tempting shape is `if !isLightPalette(th) { assertDark(th) }`. It is wrong, and worth
saying why once so nobody reaches for it: it needs a way to *know* a palette is light,
which is either a `Dark bool` field (D250 pt 5 refused it — a second copy of a fact
`Background` already states) or the same luminance read the guard was already doing. Once
you have made that read, the exemption is a tautology: "a light palette is exempt from
being dark". The test would then assert nothing at all about the two new palettes.

So the question is what the darkness rule was ever protecting, and the answer is not
darkness. Before THEME-03 kubecom painted no canvas, so a light palette's dark text landed
on the terminal's own background — genuinely broken, and D236 pt 3 was right. THEME-03
(D249) gave the palette the whole screen and THEME-04a (D250) made kubecom *report* the
case where that paint does not arrive. What survives once both of those exist is that a
palette must agree with itself: the chrome on the same side of the threshold as the canvas,
the text on the other, and the contrast floor unmoved. That holds for thirteen palettes
without naming a polarity anywhere, and a fourteenth light one needs no exemption.

One detail makes the retune safe rather than merely plausible: `IsDark` is the same
`darkLuminance` the runtime check uses (D250 pt 5). "Coherent enough to be admitted" and
"dark", as the user is told at launch, remain one number. A guard with its own threshold
could admit a palette that kubecom then describes to the reader as the other polarity.

## Solarized light does not clear the floor, and that is the leg's one real decision

`solarized-light` mirrors `solarized-dark` exactly — the scheme is designed to be read from
either end, swapping base03↔base3, base02↔base2, base01↔base1, base00↔base0 and leaving the
eight accents alone. Done canonically, the body pair is **base00 on base3: 4.13:1**, under
the 4.5 every other built-in clears. Solarized is low-contrast by design and its light end
is the lower of the two, so this is the scheme rather than a mis-mapped shade.

Three ways out, and only one is honest:

- **Lower the floor** to fit. That is deleting the half of the guard that the retune was
  supposed to keep, in the same commit that claims to keep it.
- **Invent a value** — darken base00 until it passes. Then kubecom ships a palette called
  Solarized Light containing a colour Ethan Schoonover never published, which is exactly
  what `vault/knowledge/themes.md` warns ports against.
- **Move along the ladder Solarized itself publishes.** Its light-background spec has a rung
  above body text — base01, "optional emphasized content" — at 4.99:1. Take it, and take the
  next rung for chrome text too (base02, 10.61:1).

The third is what landed, and shifting the *whole* ladder rather than one value is what
makes it a mapping choice instead of a fudge: the dark port's own relationship — chrome text
one step more emphasized than body text — is preserved, just read from one rung higher.
D251 pt 2 states the general rule so the next low-contrast port has an answer.

## Latte needed nothing, which is the interesting part

`catppuccin-latte` goes through `catppuccinTheme` completely unchanged — eleven hex values
into the same function the three dark flavors use, and the whole palette inverts. That works
because the function's roles name *rungs* of a flavor's ladder (text is the flavor's text,
mantle is the step off base the status bar sits on), and Catppuccin builds Latte on the same
rungs it builds Frappé on. If the mapping had been written in terms of shades — "a dark
background, a lighter selection" — Latte would have needed its own constructor and the
family would have stopped being one thing. Worth keeping in mind for `rose-pine-dawn`.

## What the guard still does not measure

`Header`, `Subtle` and `Primary` are unguarded, and on a light canvas they land lower than
their dark siblings: Latte's rosewater header is 2.34:1, Solarized Light's cyan 2.93:1,
against 5.37–12.95 across the dark ports. This was checked rather than assumed, and left
alone deliberately. Latte's rosewater is a pale peach — it is what Latte *is*, and
substituting a punchier hue would ship a palette under a name whose owners chose otherwise.
It is also inside the range the dark registry already tolerates for these roles: nord's
subtle is 1.69:1 and monokai's header 3.93:1. If it reads badly in front of a human the
honest fix is a different *role* for the whole Catppuccin family, not a special case for one
flavor (D251 pt 3), and `knowledge/themes.md` says so at the point someone would look.

## Verification, and what was not claimed

`make check` is green. Three mutations against the retuned guard, because a test that was
just rewritten is the one place a false green is most likely:

1. Swap `IsDark(bg.color) != canvasIsDark` to `==` → every one of the thirteen palettes
   fails, which is the sanity check that the assertion is not vacuous.
2. Set `solarized-light`'s Foreground back to the canonical base00 → the 4.5 floor fails at
   4.13, naming the pair. That is the guard the whole ladder shift exists to satisfy, so it
   had to be seen failing.
3. Give `catppuccin-latte` a dark `StatusBarBg` (Mocha's mantle) → the polarity arm fires
   ("StatusBarBg luminance 0.010 is not the same polarity as Background 0.879 — the chrome
   fights the canvas"), and the contrast arm fires behind it at 2.20:1. Both, not one: the
   dark bar under Latte's dark text is illegible as well as incoherent. The point of the
   mutation is which rule *catches* it — the old darkness rule would have **admitted** this
   bar, because a dark bar is dark, and only the contrast pair would have objected, and only
   because Latte's text happens to be dark too. The new arm names the actual defect.

What was **not** done: nobody looked at either palette on a real terminal. The mechanism
that matters for them — the canvas actually reaching the screen — is THEME-04a's, already
covered by its pty harness, and `2026-08-08-app-background-look` already asks the maintainer
to sit in front of exactly this. It was **not** amended with a ninth item: items 1-7 are
written about a dark palette on a light terminal, which is the same mismatch these two
exercise from the other side, and the task is long enough that adding a duplicate item makes
it less likely to be done rather than more. Its `Blocks:` stays `none`, and it did not gate
this leg — a palette whose values are transcribed and whose contrast is measured is not
waiting on an opinion.
