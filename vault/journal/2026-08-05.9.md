## Why a third hand-collapse was not the leg

The obvious version of this leg is the one D102 already did: collapse the list, note that it
drifted, move on. That is what makes it the wrong version. The rule has now been written down
in three places (`decisions.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, the leg skill), stated in the terms a leg reads
on every Orient, and broken by 52 legs anyway — including legs that had just read it.

The reason is structural rather than careless. A leg finishing its work has its journal entry
in hand and the board open; the cheapest correct-looking move is to paste the summary it just
wrote. Its own entry then reads perfectly well — one paragraph among two hundred lines is
invisible to the author and to any reviewer of that leg's diff. The cost lands on every
*later* Orient, and it compounds: by the time it is visible, it is 50KB of visible.

That is exactly the shape HINT-05 dealt with two legs ago, in the code: a convention that each
author can satisfy locally and no reader can check globally is not a convention, it is a hope.
The answer there was to make the incomplete case fail the gate, and the answer is the same
here. D224 pt 3 generalises it, since the precedent was already all over this repo —
the generated keybindings doc, the goreleaser changelog filters, the screencast tape — and the
vault was the one part of it enforced by nothing.

## What the guards actually check, and the one that matters

`TestBoardDoneEntriesAreOneLine` checks the shape and a 400-rune cap.
`TestBoardDoneSectionHoldsNothingButEntries` checks that the section holds nothing but entries
and blank lines. The second one is the tripwire, and it is the more important of the two: a
leg with more to say reaches for a **second line** long before it writes a 400-rune first one,
so the continuation-line check catches the drift at its first move, while the cap catches what
is already a paragraph.

The cap needed a number, and 400 is deliberately generous rather than principled. The current
median entry is ~150 runes and the longest survivor is 391; every one of the 52 offenders was
over 400. So it is set exactly where the observed failure lives, which means it rejects the
drift that happened twice and argues with nobody about the entries the board already has.
D224 pt 1 says in as many words that it is a backstop and not a target, because the failure
mode of a numeric limit is that it becomes the spec — and the reply to a too-long entry is to
shorten the entry, never to raise the number.

Runes, not bytes. These entries are full of `—`, `→` and `…`, so a byte budget would be about
a third tighter than it looks and would fail an entry the author had counted as fine.

## Collapsing 52 entries was not mechanical, and the naive version is worth naming

The first pass took everything before the first ` — ` as the short title. It is right about
two-thirds of the time and quietly wrong the rest: it produced `- [x] **M5-08** Docker`,
`- [x] **M3-15b** Edit` and `- [x] **M3-07b** Logs`, because those entries opened with a
one-word subject and put the whole claim after the dash. A Done list of one-word entries is
not shorter than the paragraphs — it is a list that has stopped answering the question the
list exists for, which is *what landed*.

So the ~25 entries whose naive title came out under 30 or over 140 runes were rewritten by
hand from the full text, keeping the ID, the date and the `(Dnn)` reference untouched in every
case. Those three fields are the join keys — the decision reference is how a reader gets from
the board to the constraint, and the id + date is how they get to the journal entry (D15) —
so the rule for the whole pass was that a collapse may lose prose and may never lose a
pointer.

## What was deliberately left, and the inconsistency found on the way

The prose above `## Done` is now the larger half of the board (~40KB of 75KB): a narrative
paragraph per closed line — SEARCH, LOGS, PAL, AUTH, CRD-PIN, HINT, BOX. It looks like the
same problem and is not. A Done entry is genuinely redundant with its journal entry, which is
why collapsing one loses nothing; a line's paragraph carries why the line was split the way it
was and what a slice deliberately left behind, and several of them are the only written record
of a constraint no `Dn` states. Compacting that is a judgement call about what is still
load-bearing, so it is filed as BOARD-02 with the conditions under which it is worth doing,
and D224 pt 2 explicitly refuses to authorise it.

The other thing the collapse surfaced: the `## Done` index has not been appended to since
2026-08-02. About a dozen legs since — AUTH-02…05b, PAL-03…05d, CRD-PIN-02…05, HINT-02…05,
BOX-01…03 — exist only as the `- [x]` line inside their own section. Both halves are defensible
(the index is canonical and wants backfilling; or the per-line entry has replaced it and the
section should say so) and the current state is neither. This leg wrote **one** entry to the
index, its own, and recorded the gap on BOARD-02 rather than backfilling: eight summaries of
legs I did not run is how a wrong one gets written down as fact.
