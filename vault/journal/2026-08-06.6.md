## Why closing human tasks came before nine pieces of feedback

The inbox is full — nine files, one of them `Priority: high` (auth errors distorting the
layout). D69 says feedback preempts the board, and it does. But the skill's pick order puts
human tasks *above* feedback, and a `Status: done` task is not work waiting to be scheduled:
it is an answer sitting in a file that every subsequent Orient re-reads and re-counts as open.
Both of these had been answered before this leg started, and the last two journal entries both
reported "nine human tasks open" — a number that was wrong when it was written.

The asymmetry that matters is cost, not priority. Folding these in is one leg; leaving them is
a small tax on every leg after it, plus the standing risk that a leg reads
`2026-07-24-edit-live-cluster-dogfood` as *open* and reports M3 as blocked on a check the
maintainer has already run. Feedback loses nothing by waiting one leg — it is a to-do list and
it stays intact.

## The two results are opposite shapes and only one of them was a tick

**Edit** is the easy one. The maintainer ran it, it worked, and the criterion says what the
criterion said. The one discipline is not to widen the words: *"editor is working"* is a
confirmation that the live suspend works — editor opens, TUI comes back, apply lands. Step 4
of that task (invalid YAML, renamed `metadata.name`, a concurrently-changed object) was never
claimed to have been exercised, and no bug was reported, which is not the same as those paths
being human-verified. So the criterion and the DoD box both say the flow is confirmed and both
still point at D129 for the reject paths. That is the D79 line and it is easy to cross by
writing "Edit is verified" full stop.

**Migration** is the interesting one, and it is why this leg produced a decision. The criterion
asked for a *real* legacy config file; the answer is that no such file exists. That is neither
"done" nor "still open" in the usual sense — the evidence is unobtainable, not merely
unproduced, and the task cannot be re-raised because asking the same person again gets the same
answer.

What made this closable honestly is that **M5-05 wrote the disposition into the task before it
knew the answer**: "If no legacy file survives, say so, and close this task with `Status: done`
and a one-line `## Result`. The criterion then closes on the generated fixture, which is the
strongest evidence obtainable — and the agent should record that it closed that way rather than
tick the box silently." The rule was set when the outcome was still open, so applying it now is
not the closing leg choosing the standard that lets it tick the box.

## Why the qualification is on the `- [x]` line and not just in the prose

D231 pt 1 is the only part of this leg I expect a future leg to actually collide with, and it
came out of reading what happens to these paragraphs over time. D229 licenses collapsing a
closed line's prose once its claims are checked; BOARD-02b-2 and -3 collapsed eight of them in
the two legs before this one. A qualification that lives only in the paragraph under a tick is
exactly the kind of sentence a well-intentioned collapse drops — it reads like history, it is
three lines long, and the tick above it will still say "Migration verified from a real legacy
config file". The `- [x]` line is the part that survives every compression, so that is where
`— ticked on the generated fixture, not on a real file (D231)` goes.

This is the same failure BOARD-02b-3 caught in the HINT line from the other direction: prose
and tick asserting different things, four lines apart. Here the tick is the thing that would
have been left over-claiming.
