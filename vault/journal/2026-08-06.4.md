## What made the answer "yes" when D224 said "not on my authority"

D224 pt 2 is the reason this was not done four legs ago, and its argument was good: the
working-area prose "carries why a line was split and what a slice deliberately left, which
the journal does *not* duplicate". Two clauses, and BOARD-02b-1 removed the second one. A
deferral now has to name a filed item, a bold `Dnn`, or "no item until asked" (D226 pt 2),
and 02b-1 walked the four paragraphs that had one and moved each to its destination — a real
item (PAL-06, since landed), a decision (D227), a declined answer with reasons, or an
until-asked. So "what a slice deliberately left" is no longer *in* those paragraphs; it is in
the places an Orient already reads.

That leaves the first clause, and it does not survive contact with the Done index. Every
slice has an entry, every entry carries the slice id, and the id is the join key to the
journal file that holds the split rationale in full (D15). `PAL-05 was split on pickup
because unused is in the lint gate and a picker has 20–52 references` is a sentence about a
leg that ran on 2026-08-02; it is in that leg's journal entry, at more length and with the
count that justified it. Keeping a second copy on the board is the same shape as the Done
paragraphs D102 and D224 collapsed — the leg that writes it pays nothing, every Orient after
pays for it.

## The collapse is not new — three sections already read this way

The thing that settled it is that the board already does this and nobody wrote it down. M1,
M2 and M4 are one italic sentence each: `_(none — M2 is **done** (2026-07-29): every exit
criterion … is ticked against named evidence (M2-EXIT/D154) …)_`. Outcome, date, pointer.
Those lines had exactly as much history behind them as SEARCH or PAL — M2 is thirty-odd
slices — and no leg has ever wanted it back.

So the shape was available all along; what the feedback-driven lines lacked was a leg whose
job was to close them, because a feedback line closes when its last slice lands and the leg
that lands it is thinking about the slice. That is the asymmetry, and it is why D229 pt 1
names the collapse as a normal part of closing a line rather than as a cleanup pass.

## What I would not let the collapse drop

Two categories, and only the first is obvious.

**Standing answers.** SEARCH's field selector is *declined* with a reason (per-kind field
support varies, a selector the kind does not support fails its List, a failed kind is silent
by design — D131 pt 3, so it would quietly drop most of the scope). CRD-PIN has two
`no item until asked`s, one of which argues with D202 pt 4. PAL has two. Collapsing a
paragraph that holds one of these does not just lose prose — it converts a considered "no"
into an unanswered question, and the next leg looking for something unblocked files it. D226
pt 3 was written to make these legible on Orient; dropping them in the name of brevity would
undo that decision four days after it was made. So every one of them is in the collapsed
text, and the collapsed AUTH paragraph still says "nothing is deferred here" *explicitly*,
because the absence of an answer and an answer of "none" read differently.

**Constraints that are only there.** These should already be gone — D226 pt 2 sends them to
`decisions.md` — but "should" is what D224 said about one-line Done entries. I checked each
of the four before touching it: SEARCH's `searchScatteredShare = limit/4` guess is recorded
in D191 pt 2 (verbatim enough — "a later leg may not cite this closure as evidence for
keeping them *or* for changing them"), CRD-PIN's is D227, PAL's marker rule is D228 pt 3,
AUTH's detect-narrowly/never-run-unasked is D195 pt 4/5. All four were citations by the time
I read them, which is what made these four the safe set. It is also why the collapsed text
keeps every `Dnn` it found: the pointer is the whole remaining value of the sentence.

## Why the other four are an item and not the rest of this leg

LOGS, DIAG, HINT and BOX are closed and were never swept. Two of them are visibly stale in
the direction D226 pt 1 predicts: HINT's closing paragraph says "Nothing enforces the
completeness, which is D218 pt 1" and three lines below it another paragraph says HINT-05
enforced it (D223) — the board asserts both. DIAG's spends fourteen lines restating D191 pt
1, which is a decision written for exactly this purpose.

Collapsing those without checking them first would be the failure mode D229 pt 3 names:
a stale claim in a long paragraph is at least visibly hedged and dated, while the same claim
compressed into "the DIAG line is closed, CRD-01 is a client bug fixed at D200" is short,
confident and wrong. So the sweep is the work, the collapse is its last step, and both belong
to BOARD-02b-3. It is also the leg-sizing answer: this leg's diff is ~300 lines with four
sections; eight would have been ~450.

## No guard this time, and that is a decision rather than an omission

The three BOARD legs before this one each ended in an `internal/vault` test, and the pull to
end this one the same way was strong — a byte cap on the working area, or a per-section line
budget. D229 pt 4 refuses it. Every guard those legs added states a rule with no judgement in
it: a Done entry is one line; the Done section holds nothing but entries; a finished item is
in the index; a deferral names its destination. "This sentence is no longer load-bearing" is
not that kind of rule, and a size cap would be satisfied by writing less about **open** lines
— the CTX-WARM and BOARD sections, the M5 preamble — which is the prose the board exists to
carry. The check that actually holds this rule is the one D229 pt 1 puts on the leg that
closes a line: collapse the section then, when you are the one who knows what it said.
